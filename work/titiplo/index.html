TITIPLO

A mobile-first operating system designed to help jastip sellers manage trips, products, buyers, orders, payments, and recurring business workflows from one place.

RoleProduct / Operations / Build
PlatformSeller App + Buyer Storefront
ArchitectureServerless
StatusReleased & iterating
01 — THE PROBLEM

A business model built on trust, but operated through fragmented tools.

Jastip sellers often coordinate trips, product availability, customer requests, payment proof, purchase progress, and shipping through chat plus spreadsheets. That works at small scale, but the operational load grows quickly.

FRICTION 01

Scattered order context

Important information can live across multiple conversations and manual records.

FRICTION 02

Manual status updates

Seller and buyer visibility can depend on repeated chat updates instead of a shared flow.

FRICTION 03

Hard to scale trust

A public-facing product needs clearer structure, status, and transaction evidence than a close-circle jastip workflow.

02 — PRODUCT PRINCIPLE

Do not replace the jastiper. Make the jastiper easier to trust and easier to operate.

The product direction was intentionally seller-first. The platform had to preserve the personal nature of jastip while reducing administrative friction and giving buyers a clearer storefront and order journey.

PRINCIPLE

Mobile first

The seller workflow is designed around a phone, because the business happens while sellers are moving, sourcing, and communicating.

PRINCIPLE

Minimal infrastructure

Use lightweight serverless components to keep the product inexpensive to operate and simple to evolve.

PRINCIPLE

Guided workflow

Status, actions, and navigation should reduce the amount of business logic the seller has to remember manually.

03 — ARCHITECTURE

Lean infrastructure with clear ownership of each job.

The system separates the interface, business logic, structured data, and uploaded proof files so each layer stays understandable.

The goal was not maximum technical complexity. The goal was an architecture that could support a real product while keeping cost and maintenance manageable.

04 — CORE FLOWS

Two experiences, one shared transaction state.

The seller side focuses on operational control. The buyer side focuses on confidence, visibility, and a simpler purchase journey.

Seller flow

1
Create trip

Define destination, currency, schedule, and selling context.

2
Add products

Manage pricing, markup, images, and availability per trip.

3
Manage orders

Track customers, order items, proof of payment, and seller actions.

4
Purchase & handoff

Move the transaction through purchase and shipping or hand-carry completion.

Buyer flow

1
Browse storefront

Discover available trip products from a public buyer-facing experience.

2
Place order

Submit item selection with clearer structure than a chat-only request.

3
Submit payment proof

Attach transaction evidence to the same order context.

4
Follow progress

See transaction updates without relying entirely on manual seller messages.

05 — ITERATION

The product improved through operational details, not only visual polish.

A large part of the work was refining behavior after using the product: navigation, loading states, seller actions, payment flows, notification destinations, account settings, and subscription infrastructure.

Seller workflow foundation

Trips, products, customers, orders, purchase, shipping, and file-proof handling.

Buyer storefront

A separate buyer-facing experience connected to the seller's operational data.

Identity & account layer

Login, registration, OTP verification, password reset, seller account settings, and session behavior.

Subscription layer

Free-plan logic, upgrade flows, subscription history, and manual-payment-ready infrastructure.

Release polish

Navigation behavior, loading feedback, sticky headers, button interaction states, and removal of internal technical messaging from the user experience.

OUTCOME

A working product system instead of a portfolio-only concept.

TITIPLO became a practical exercise in combining product thinking, operations knowledge, UX decisions, backend architecture, mobile behavior, and cost constraints into one evolving SaaS product.
